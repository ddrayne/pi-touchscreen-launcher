# The poster frame — design

Date: 2026-10-17

## Brief

The idle frame is tasteful but static: cream paper, serif, oval portraits.
The owner wants it radically better from a visual point of view, combining
three things: living data-art, real photographs so the screen still says
"this is the bird", and bold poster energy with strong blocks. The chosen
direction is that **the poster is generated by the birds**: the layout, the
block sizes and the colours all come from what BirdFrame heard.

Decisions taken in the brainstorm:

- The poster is generated from the data, not a fixed layout with a
  visualisation inside it.
- Colour comes from both the time of day (ground and type) and the birds
  (each block tinted from its own portrait).
- When the hour is quiet the window widens to today, labelled honestly.
- Clock, weather, forecast, coming up and the house list are blocks in the
  same grid. The whole panel is one poster.
- Performance first: nothing renders continuously when nothing happens.

## 1. The composition

- 1200 × 1920 portrait. A 6 × 10 grid of near-square cells (about 170 px),
  16 px gutters, 48 px outer margin. Every block snaps to whole cells.
- Rows 1–2 are the house: clock 3 × 2, weather now 2 × 2, date and honesty
  line 1 × 2. Rows 9–10: coming up 4 × 1, house list 2 × 1, forecast 6 × 1.
  These positions are fixed.
- Rows 3–8 (36 cells) are the birds. Weight per species is roughly
  `1 + log2(calls)` scaled by recency. A packer turns weights into blocks
  between 1 × 1 and 4 × 4. The latest bird is guaranteed at least 3 × 3.
  Species that don't fit collapse into one "and N more" block.
- Reading order is time order. The blocks are dealt newest first, the latest
  bird at the top left, each at the first place after the last one's origin
  where its shape fits, so reading the field left to right and down is
  reading the hour newest to oldest. Each block says how long ago ("just
  now", "12 min ago"); the newest bird's is the one gold word on the field.
- Between events nothing moves or changes size, however the ink cools. The
  field is dealt afresh only when a bird is heard, a bird leaves the window,
  or the window changes; the deal is a FLIP transition. Sizes carry a one-shape
  hysteresis from the previous field so a re-deal does not jiggle the rest.
  Each block leans tall or wide by its name, so the same garden gives the
  same arrangement across reboots.
- Fewer than four species in the hour: draw from today instead; the honesty
  line reads "9 voices today · quiet for 25 min".

## 2. The bird block

- A hard-cropped photograph filling the block, a tint from that photograph,
  type on top. No borders, no ovals.
- 1 × 1: solid tint and the name. 2 × 1 / 1 × 2: photo one half, tint and
  name the other. 2 × 2 and up: full photo, tint gradient from the bottom,
  name large across the lower third, then time · tier · one dot per call.
- The bridge extracts a dominant tint and a focal point when it caches a
  portrait and serves them with the credit. The focal point drives the crop.
  Fallback: ground colour and centre crop.
- Recency is ink: as the last call ages the photo desaturates and the tint
  sinks towards the ground, until a species unheard for forty minutes is a
  quiet pale square. A fresh call snaps it back.
- Tier is type weight: confirmed heavy, probable regular, tentative light
  italic. The word still appears in the small line.
- New to this garden: thin gold inner frame and the label above the name.
- Touch a bird block to hear its clip: gold ring, dots light in sequence,
  one clip at a time, wake word muted while it plays.

## 3. How it lives

- Idle: only the clock tick and one very slow ground drift animate.
- Arrival: the block flashes to full tint, a ring expands and fades, a dot
  drops in. A size change is a FLIP transition on grid placement (transforms
  only, ~900 ms). A new species takes its place in time, the others shuffle
  along in order.
- Cooling: once a minute each block's age sets a CSS variable with a long
  transition.
- Breathing: the activity buckets set one tempo that drives gutter width and
  the ground grain's drift speed. One number, one animation duration.
- Ground: the existing solar palette. Dawn rose and apricot, day paper, dusk
  amber into plum, night deep navy with cream type. Sun, moon and stars show
  through gutters and margins. At night photos dim to ~60% under their tint.
- Unreachable: the poster freezes, cools fully over ten minutes, the honesty
  line says "BirdFrame unreachable · last heard HH:MM".

## 4. House blocks and the touched pages

- House blocks sit on a shade of the ground colour, type in ink. Clock
  numerals cut tight to the block. Weather: big temperature and one mark.
  Forecast: five columns. Coming up: next two items, bin days in gold. House
  list: "9 things to pick up".
- Touch on the idle poster: bird block plays; clock, weather, forecast open
  Household; coming up and house list open Household at that panel; margins
  and gutters wake Voice.
- Voice page: same grid, a 6 × 3 voice block at the top, then today's birds
  as blocks sized by today's counts, then shopping and Look. Household page:
  agenda, rooms, shopping and the painting of the day as blocks. Tab bar
  stays. Shopping list and camera keep their layouts, take the type and
  palette.
- Dropped: the "Earlier today" strip of ovals, the roll call, the gallery.

## 5. Build and test

- `lib/frame/poster.ts`: `composePoster(snapshot, agenda, weather, now,
  previous)` → blocks with kind, grid area, weight, age, tint. Packer,
  widening rule, minimum-size guarantee, stability, overflow are pure.
- `lib/frame/palette.ts`: ground colours from solar state; cooling curve.
- `tests/frame-poster.test.mjs`: loud hour fills 36 cells with no overlap or
  gap; quiet hour widens; the field reads newest to oldest; nothing moves
  between events; a bird heard again goes to the top and the rest follow;
  deterministic; sizes within 1 × 1 … 4 × 4.
- Bridge: portrait cache gains `tint` and `focal`, computed once with Pillow;
  omitted when Pillow is missing.
- `components/frame/Poster.tsx` renders the grid and runs FLIP on area
  changes; small block components per kind, reused by Voice and Household.
- Verification: mock-bridge fixtures for a loud afternoon, a quiet evening, a
  new-to-garden arrival and unreachable; screenshots by day, dusk and night;
  deploy and compare idle CPU with the figure from `9c70df7`.
